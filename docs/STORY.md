# Kigu Quest — Story Bible (owner: Writer)

*Companion to `docs/BIBLE.md` (world, roster, outline) and `docs/text.md` (how text is keyed and budgeted). Where this file and the BIBLE
disagree on a fact of the world, the BIBLE wins; where they disagree on a name I invented, this file wins. Proposed names for places the
level designers have not built yet are marked (proposed) — `docs/levels.md` is authoritative for anything on a map.*

## 1. Pitch and tone

> A land stitched together out of friendships is about to be ironed flat by the person who loves it most.
> A kid with a thimble of courage, a childhood friend who is also a rival, a cheerful friend with a bottle of fabric softener, and eight very different
> Master Tailors have to show one grieving woman that a patch is not a flaw.

**Tone: warm, funny, cosy, with real stakes.**

* **Warm.** Everyone is kind first. Even the villains are polite, and have a reason. Nobody is humiliated for who they are.
* **Funny.** Wit comes from character and specifics (a named snack, an oddly precise hobby, a verbal tic), not from cruelty. At most one
  sewing pun per scene, and sometimes the speaker apologises for it ("...I'll see myself out").
* **Cosy.** Tea, cocoa, laundry on the line, lamplight, festival ribbons, the clack of a loom. Every town has a place to sit down.
* **Real stakes.** Pressed Kigu with dimmed eyes. A festival about to be flattened. A family split for thirty years. The fear is quiet, never gory.
* **All-ages.** No fanservice, no gore, no death (Kigu "fall asleep"; "lost" means "separated", and everyone is found). No swearing
  ("darn" is the strongest word in Tsumugi). Never snark at the player; teasing is for friends and only goes one way: up.
* **The player never speaks.** They nod (yes/no prompts). Other characters talk *to* them: "you", or {player} when narrating.
  Never gender the player (no he/she); Kigu are always "she", the rival is never gendered in player-facing text (they/them in docs), Mimi is "she".

**Craft rules for every line:** (1) vary rhythm — a short line after a long one; (2) the last line of a box carries the joke or the feeling;
(3) be specific ("a lemon macaron", not "a snack"); (4) one verbal tic per character, used sparingly; (5) people talk about what they
*do*, not about the plot; (6) don't explain the joke; (7) 2 lines per box, ~75 characters — if it doesn't fit, cut, don't cram.

**Dialogue style requirements (owner, 2026-10-25; these override any "laconic" or "five words" voice note below):**

Write dialogue that sounds like something a real person would spontaneously say, not like stylized prose arranged into dialogue-shaped sentences.
Natural speech takes priority over brevity, atmosphere, and poetic phrasing.

* Prefer complete, conversational sentences. Use sentence fragments only when they have a clear purpose, such as hesitation, interruption, emphasis, uncertainty, or a character's established manner of speaking.
* Do not automatically shorten sentences to make dialogue sound gritty, mysterious, dramatic, ancient, or profound.
* Avoid consecutive short declarative sentences that could naturally be combined into one flowing thought.
* Use ordinary connective words, contractions, and conversational phrasing where appropriate. Characters should be able to explain themselves without sounding like they are reciting an inscription.
* Avoid cryptic metaphors, lyrical comparisons, and poetic shorthand unless they suit the individual character, situation, and setting.
* Do not confuse a character being knowledgeable or reserved with a character speaking unnaturally.
* Give each character a distinct voice through their vocabulary, temperament, knowledge, priorities, and conversational habits, rather than forcing every character to speak in the same clipped style.
* Allow dialogue to breathe. People sometimes elaborate, qualify what they mean, repeat a word for emphasis, or add a small clarification without it being unnecessary exposition.
* Read every line aloud mentally. If it sounds theatrical, artificially profound, or unnatural in ordinary speech, rewrite it.
* Preserve subtext and avoid overexplaining, but do not sacrifice natural sentence structure merely to make a line shorter.

Revision test: Before finalizing dialogue, identify sentences that feel like isolated fragments or decorative metaphors. Rewrite them into natural speech unless there is a specific character-driven reason to retain them.
Do not make every character verbose or casual. The goal is believable speech appropriate to the character and setting, not generic modern conversation.

**Metaphor and Simile Quality Control (owner, 2026-10-25; applies to dialogue, narration and signs):**

Do not generate metaphors, similes, or poetic comparisons merely to make prose sound literary.
Every comparison must communicate a recognizable quality that the reader can understand without inventing an explanation for the author.
Before using a metaphor or simile, ask:

1. What exact quality am I describing?
2. What concrete, recognizable property of the comparison illustrates that quality?
3. Would a reader naturally understand the connection without additional context?

Reject comparisons that connect unrelated qualities, combine sensory details without a clear purpose, or sound evocative while conveying little meaningful information.
For example, "slow as warm sand" is not acceptable without specific context establishing why warm sand represents slowness. Temperature alone does not establish speed.
Prefer precise, concrete language over decorative imagery. Do not force a metaphor where an ordinary description communicates the idea more clearly.
Use figurative language when it reveals something about the character, setting, emotion, or experience. Do not use it simply because a sentence seems too plain.
A beautiful-sounding sentence that does not make sense is a failed sentence.


## 2. Themes (and where you can see them)

1. **Individuality vs. uniformity.** The land is a *patchwork*; the Starch Society wants a *plain white weave*. Every Kigu costume is
   different; a Pressed Kigu's is bleached white with blue piping. The hero ends with eight *different* Buttons on one jacket.
2. **Bonds you choose.** Kigu *choose* their Tailors (never captured: she shakes the spool because she is deciding). The rival and Mimi choose to stay
   friends. The twins choose. The Society's sin is taking the choosing away.
3. **Mending, not erasing.** You don't iron out a tear, you mend it — visibly, proudly (the Weave Guardian's coat is all patches). The villain is not
   destroyed; she is *invited to sew again*. Pressing is a fine tool for a seam; it is a terrible philosophy for a world.

Motif kit: buttons, threads, patches, tea, tape measures, lanterns, white vs. colour, "where everyone can see" (visible mending).

## 3. The world in one minute

**Tsumugi** ("spun thread") is a patchwork land joined by *seam roads*. **Kigu** are spirit-girls who wear *living costumes* (animal, plant,
object onesies that grow more elaborate as they evolve). A person who forms a **Stitch Bond** with a Kigu is a **Tailor**. The
**Global Tailor's Association** is the authority over every Tailor, and it is led by **the Needles**. In Tsumugi its local branch, the
**Tailors' Guild** (proposed: the Guild is the Association's Tsumugi branch), licenses Tailors, runs the eight **Salons** (each led by a
**Master Tailor**, who gives a **Button**), and crowns the Weave Guardian at the **Grand Atelier**. **The Needles have vanished**, and the
**Starch Society**, which everyone trusted, stepped into the gap they left. The Society believes the world should be pressed flat.

**Timeline.** *Before the land:* Astara and Anwela, the Goddesses of Order and Nature, weave a magic tapestry to hold the **Unweaving**
(working name, §7a), who threatened to consume the universe. *Long ago:* they weave Tsumugi as the tapestry's opposite, to keep him
contained. *Thirty years ago:* **the Great Snarl** at Loomhaven's Lantern Festival (§6); Hester's ribbon wardens grow into the **Order of
Ribbons**, a respected Tailor organisation.
*A few years ago (proposed: five):* the Needles vanish, the Order's leader is tricked by the Unweaving, and the Order reorganises
into the **Starch Society**, which fills the power vacuum. *Now:* the hero is 12 or 13, turns apprentice, and the Society is weeks from its big day: **this year's Lantern Festival.**

## 4. The cast

### The protagonists: Dahlia and Draco
Dahlia and Draco grew up next door to each other in Button Town. Their back gardens share a fence with a washing line that has tangled both their kites at least twice. They have one tree house between them, with one wobbly plank that only one of them will admit to falling through. They swapped lunch boxes once and never agreed who won. Every Saturday they raced to the bakery, usually to a tie. The kite that got stuck in the Salon banner in Thimble is still up there. That is why the banner hangs "two degrees crooked", and neither of them has ever said sorry.

**Who they are.** The one you play is the steady one: careful with a needle, the first to stop and help a lost Kigu, the one who notices. The other is the rival: sunny, a bit show-offy, loyal, quick to laugh, and sulky for exactly three seconds after a loss. Dahlia is the default name for the girl and Draco for the boy. You can rename either of them. Whichever one you do not play is always the rival.

**Why they both set out.** Professor Bobbin takes both of them on as apprentices on the same morning, because they have been turning up at her lab since they could walk. Both want to see the whole of Tsumugi. From the top of the tree house, years ago, they promised each other they would see every Salon together. Neither of them wants to arrive second. Both of them will cheer the other one on when they do.

### The hero ({player}, boy or girl)
Lives with Mom in Button Town; the tailor's overalls (flat cap for the boy, red beret for the girl), the spool-pouch belt and the satchel. Wants "a Kigu of my own, someday" — and gets one on page one. A steady
needle: the hero's gift is not power but *noticing and stopping to help*, which is exactly what a Stitch Bond needs. **Arc:** apprentice → the person who
mends the land. Never speaks; the writing gives them things to be kind *to*.

### {rival} — the rival (Draco if you play Dahlia, Dahlia if you play Draco)
Sunny, a bit show-offy, loyal, quick to laugh. Hates losing for exactly three seconds, then cheers the winner loudest. Teases the player *up*, never down; no insults, no sulking, no "Hmph." Drawn with the other hero's look (no look of its own). Their dad is a floor manager at **Crisp & Co.** in Seamstead, and his letters home have been getting *so tidy lately*. Their mum is warm and a little worried about those letters, and the rival is worried too, though they do not say so yet.
**Arc:** (1) Lab: you both get a Kigu on the same morning. The rival takes the one that beats yours, as a dare: "Fair is fair!" (2) Route 1: the first friendly challenge, at the top of Meadow Lane. (3) Thimble: loses to Poppy and turns up outside the Salon with snack cakes and tips. (4) Seamstead: defends Crisp & Co. because their dad works there; the raid shakes them. (5) Quarry: quieter; asks for a battle "to measure" how far you have both come. (6) Kite Hill: goes looking for their dad; Dart teases them until they laugh. (7) The Starch Works: finds their dad pressed on the Pressing Floor, and gets him back. (8) The Last Seam: the friendly final fight: *"You were never in my way. You were the map."* They ask Prof. Bobbin to take them on as an apprentice. They win their Buttons on their own, never become Weave Guardian, and do not need to.

### Mimi (the cheerful friend)
Pink cardigan, enormous bow, talks in exclamation marks, always has a spare snack. Dreams of *designing Kigu costumes*, not battling. She brings the
hero a Silk Spool in Thimble, finds the first Pressed Kigu with them in the Woods, and invents **Soft Rinse** (fabric softener, basically) — the only thing
that un-presses a Kigu. **Arc:** cheerful sidekick → the quiet hero of the Works (drives the delivery cart that opens the back gate, unpresses a hundred Kigu)
→ Loomhaven's hero the morning after: she leads the townspeople with buckets. Running gag: "Did my bow get bigger? People keep saying it got bigger."

### Professor Bobbin
Elderly, round glasses, tape-measure scarf, lab coat full of pencils. Studies Kigu ("I do not study people, which is why I am so bad at them"). Forever
searching for her tape measure (it is around her neck). Gives the starters and the Sketchbook. **Secret:** she is **Madame Damask's younger sister** (born
Bobbin; Hester took the Guild's title "Damask" when she became Guildmistress). They have not spoken since the Snarl, each believing the other blames her.
Seeds: a photo on her desk of two laughing girls, one tidy, one not; "my sister folded everything in thirds"; the Quarry is named for their family.
Reveals in Loomhaven.
**The errand that starts the journey (owner pick, 2026-10-26).** Bobbin has been studying the Needles' disappearance and thinks they left
a trail. Every Master Tailor's Button is sewn on with that Master's own thread, and she suspects the eight threads together matter
somehow, though she cannot say why yet. So she sends the hero (and the rival) to collect each Master's thread along with the Button.
Neither of them knows what it is for until the Great Loom, where the hero braids all eight and ties the twins together. This gives the
early game a real goal while everyone knows the Needles are gone: becoming Weave Guardian is not the reason to set out, it is where the
road ends up. Her line to the hero at the start: "Go and see Tsumugi. Meet the Masters. The threads will show you the rest."

### Mom, {rival}'s mum, the town
Mom bakes and heals you with cocoa. {rival}'s mum has known you since you were both in nappies, and treats you like family. She worries about her husband's tidy letters. Neighbours are warm, nosy and slightly over-informed about other people's Kigu.

**Why the Seamstresses chose the hero (owner, 2026-10-26).** Mom has an older Kigu who helps around the house (owner approved 2026-10-26: a
**Nibblenna**, the baker hamster, since Mom bakes). Like every Kigu, she can talk with the Seamstresses, and she has been bragging about
the hero to them for years. The hero has always shown Kigu empathy, kindness and friendship, so the Seamstresses have had their eye on
them for the day they are old enough to become a Tailor. That is why most Kigu are so drawn to the hero: they can sense the Seamstresses'
favour on them. It is also how word of the hero reaches the Needles inside the tapestry (§7b). Proposed seeds: Mom's Kigu greets the
hero like an old friend and hums at the window at night; wild Kigu linger near the hero more than near other kids (a line from Bobbin:
"They don't usually do that.").

### "Mr. Sharp" — the recurring peddler (spoiler: Sharps, the fourth Needle, who took Damask's old seat)
A cheerful travelling seller in a different absurd disguise in every town (false moustache, a chef's hat, a sailor cap) who always insists he is "just a humble peddler,
nothing suspicious." Everyone sees through him; he doesn't know. He is the Guild's scout, and it is his report that makes the Weave Guardian say *yes*.
Catchphrase: "Keep your eyes sharp, friend!" (NPC id suggestion `peddler`, in Thimble, Hemline, Seamstead, the Quarry, Kite Hill, Woolen Peak, Loomhaven, the Last Seam.)

## 6. The Starch Society

**Mantra:** *"Flat is fair."* **Motto:** *"Perfection is just the beginning."* (Both are the owner's words; keep them verbatim. The mantra
is what Pressers say to each other and to you; the motto is on the banners, the letterheads and Damask's lips.)

**What they believe:** *Flat is fair. Crisp. Clean. Even.* A cloth that is perfectly flat cannot tangle or tear. So: no wild Kigu (they are "creases"), no tangles, no surprises. A
**Pressed** Kigu is bleached white with blue piping, calm, obedient, and says "Yes, Tailor." in a pleasant monotone. She cannot choose. (Soft Rinse undoes it.)

**How it started (their sympathetic motive).** Thirty years ago, at the **Loomhaven Lantern Festival**, a storm-wind snatched the ribbon-lanterns and knotted them through the crowd: **the Great Snarl**.
Nobody was hurt badly, but hundreds of children and Kigu were scattered for a night, and the Guildmistress — the sisters' mother — lost the strength in her hands and never sewed again. Her elder
daughter **Hester**, then a festival warden, took the Guild's title *Madame Damask* and made a vow: *never again.* Her first step was kind — "ribbon wardens" who tied every child to a parent with an
identical ribbon. The wardens grew into the **Order of Ribbons** (owner, 2026-10-26), a respected Tailor organisation that ran lost-and-found desks, festival safety and
Tailor patrols. The idea grew: identical ribbons, identical costumes, identical roads. Many of its members joined because they were frightened, and were *helped*. That is why it works.
Her younger sister **Bobbin** went the other way: if wild threads can tangle, study them. They have not spoken since.

**How it went wrong (owner, 2026-10-26).** For most of those thirty years the Order of Ribbons really was benevolent. Then the Needles
vanished (§7b), and around the same time the Order's leader, Damask, was **tricked by the Unweaving** (how is still open, §15). She
**reorganised the Order from top to bottom into the Starch Society**: new uniforms, new rules, new name. Tailors who had nobody else
to ask started asking the Society. It was organised, polite and already everywhere, so it filled the power vacuum almost without trying.
That is why there are Society inspectors on bridges in Chapter 1 and why nobody argues with them.

**Two plans, one Society.** *What the members believe:* they are simply making the world more orderly. Tidy roads, no tangles, nobody
lost. Pressers and most Pleats are sincere about this. *The real plan:* press Tsumugi flat and **merge it with the tapestry to free the
Unweaving**. Proposed: only Damask knows the whole of it; the Pleats each know one piece.

**The lie they were told.** The Unweaving's story, which the leadership believes and teaches, is that **the seamstresses are the bad
ones**: they locked him in the tapestry to keep his power for themselves, and freeing him is a rescue. Damask believed it because it
gave her fear a purpose. They have been lied to, and only the top of the Society is passing the lie on.

**Damask is an ex-Needle (owner, 2026-10-26).** After the Snarl, Hester rose from Guildmistress to a seat among the Needles. Every Needle
carries a needle threaded with **Everspool** silk as the badge of the office (proposed), which is why the Society has a piece of the
Everspool at all. Proposed: she held the seat for about twenty years, then gave it up to run the Order of Ribbons full time, and kept her
thread. Sharps, the youngest Needle, took her seat. Because the tapestry is woven of the same thread, the loose thread found her through
it, and it is also how the Unweaving reached the other Needles. She had already left, so she did not vanish with them; she was simply the
one he could still talk to. Her Needle name was **Chenille** (owner approved 2026-10-26), a real needle like the others.

**Madame Damask.** Tall, iron-grey bun, steam-iron brooch, exquisitely polite ("Do mind the creases, dear"). Never raises her voice. Pours tea. Her weakness: she has not sewn a stitch since
the Snarl (her mother's thimble is in Rue's drawer). Her plan: **re-weave Tsumugi as a plain weave** — over, under, over, under, no patterns — using the **Great Press** at the Starch Works and the
twins' threads pulled taut through the **Great Loom** in Loomhaven during the Lantern Festival, then pass the pressed weave into the magic
tapestry, which she believes will set its prisoner free: *"This year, nobody gets lost."* Her ace is the twin the hero did not save.

**The Pleats (admins)** — each with a human reason, each beaten kindly:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pleat Crease** | Gingham Woods, Seamstead | 20s, brisk, folding ruler, straightens everything she passes | Eldest of nine; the Society gave her a uniform that was *hers* and a desk that stayed tidy | "Not one crease out of place!" |
| **Pleat Serge** | Crisp & Co. (Seamstead), the Cellar | 40s, silky salesman, never stops smiling, genuinely pays fair wages | His family's tailor shop failed because every garment came out different | "Quality is consistency." |

**Pressers** (grunts, `grunt_m` / `grunt_f`): white uniforms, blue starch collars, flat caps. They say "Flat is fair!" and "Stay in line." Several are obviously homesick, one wants to bake,
one keeps folding your shirt while you battle. Class key `presser`. **Crisp & Co.:** the respectable front — linens, uniforms, "Crisp Care" cleaning. **Starch Works:** the old Woolen Peak mill.
**Public face:** "The Starch Society: A Society for Tidy Living." Its Tidy Days are folded-laundry parades.

## 7. The legend, the twins, the Needles and the Weave Guardian

**The true beginning (owner, 2026-10-26; nobody alive tells it this way).** Astara and Anwela were the **Goddesses of Order and Nature**
(proposed: Astara is Order, the steady straight thread; Anwela is Nature, the restless thread that wanders across). The Unweaving
threatened to consume the universe, so they wove a **magic tapestry** to contain him, using the **Everspool** (owner confirmed 2026-10-26). Then they wove **Tsumugi as the tapestry's
opposite**, so he would be better contained: the tapestry is tight, flat and still, and Tsumugi is loose, patched and full of choices.
As long as the two stay different, they cannot become one cloth, and he cannot get out.

**Why the goddesses are Kigu (owner, 2026-10-26).** They made the first Kigu by weaving spirits of nature into living costumes with the
Everspool's thread. Doing that bound the goddesses to the same fate, and they became Kigu themselves. It is why they can speak through
any Kigu: all Kigu are woven from the same thread they are. *That is why flattening Tsumugi matters:* the
flatter and more uniform it gets, the more it looks like the tapestry, until the two can be merged. (Their names are **Astara** (Order) and **Anwela** (Nature), from the Welsh *ystof* "warp" and *anwe* "weft"; owner,
2026-10-26. Say them "uh-STAR-uh" and "an-WEL-uh". The game code still uses the old ids `warpa` / `weftie` until a rename pass.)

**The legend of Tsumugi (as told in Loomhaven's murals and Tapestry's lullaby):** In the beginning there was only *fluff* — loose, drifting, nameless. (This is what was left over once
the tapestry was finished.) Two sisters woke in it. **Astara** could pull a
thread straight; **Anwela** could carry one across. "Hold this end," said Astara. "I'll bring the other," said Anwela. Up and across they went, and where a thread crossed a thread they tied a
knot, and where they tied a knot a town grew (the first knot is Button Town — *the first button*). They dressed the wild spirits in living cloth so nobody would be lonely: the Kigu. When people
came, the sisters said: *"No thread holds by itself. Let the people and the Kigu choose one another."* That was the first Stitch Bond. When the weave was done they were tired, and lay down at the two
ends of the cloth — Astara in a bed of embers in the north (Woolen Peak), Anwela in a bed of tides at the sea (Loomhaven harbour) — and promised to wake if the weave ever tore.

**The seamstresses speak through Kigu (owner, 2026-10-26).** Astara and Anwela are the **legendary Kigu seamstresses**. They do not talk
the way people do, but every Kigu wears cloth they wove, so they can speak *through* a Kigu. That is how they talk to the hero in the
end-game: a line appears in the hero's lead Kigu's text box, under her name, but the words are the sisters'. Before that, the only hint
is a Kigu who briefly says something she could not know (proposed: once after the twin choice in Chapter 8).

**Lullaby (Tea House, Tapestry, Loomhaven):** *"Warp goes up and weft goes by, / one holds fast and one runs wide. / Cross them once and tie them true, / and the world will hold for me and you."*

**The truth the Society missed:** the goddess of Order is on the hero's side. Real order is a weave that holds because its threads can *give*;
the Society's order is a copy of the tapestry. A weave only holds if its threads can *give*. The Great Press works by pulling the sisters taut *apart* — the tension is what flattens the land. Reunite them, and
the tension slackens and colour returns.

**Astara** (Dream/Ember, white-and-crimson robe, vertical threads from her sleeves): steady, patient, remembers everything. **Anwela** (Dream/Tide, white-and-blue, horizontal ribbons): curious, restless,
always wants to see what's over there. At the Works they are held in two tension frames. **The hero can free only one** (cut her thread with the Disc: Snip Poppy gave you — the first gift pays off):
the one freed takes the hero's thread in her sleeve and joins the party (suggest Lv 45ish). Damask takes the other to Loomhaven. The text supports both (`{twin}` / `{twin2}`; story keys end `.warpa` / `.weftie`). The rival stands beside the hero for this choice: *"Whichever you pick, I have got your back."*

### 7a. The Unweaving (working name) and the magic tapestry

**What it is (owner, 2026-10-26).** The real enemy of the game is **not a Kigu**. It is a non-Kigu entity that is the antithesis of the
legendary seamstresses: they tie threads together and let people and Kigu choose each other, and he pulls threads apart until there is
nothing left to choose. Left loose, he would consume the universe (owner). Pronoun: he. It has no costume and no Sketchbook page. **It cannot be bonded
with**: a Bond Spool simply will not take, and the battle text says so plainly. **The Everspool cannot sway it** either. The Everspool's
thread can calm any heart that is able to choose, which is how the first Stitch Bond was tied; the entity has nothing to choose with,
so the thread slides off.

**The tapestry.** Before Tsumugi existed, the goddesses could not unmake him, so they wove a magic tapestry around him, and then wove
Tsumugi as its opposite to keep him in (§7). The tapestry is woven from Everspool thread, like the Kigu. The tapestry holds him, but he is not quiet.

**How he speaks to the Society (owner, 2026-10-26).** One **loose thread** of the tapestry found its way out into Tsumugi. He talks through
it, the same way the goddesses talk through Kigu, because it is the same thread. That loose thread is how he reached the Order's leader and
how he still gives the Society its orders. (Proposed: Damask keeps it wound on a plain white spool in her brooch case, and the Society's
interest in the Everspool in Chapter 2 is really an attempt to find more of the same thread.) Its pull is what made the Needles vanish (proposed: they were drawn into the tapestry, §7 below).

**Name and look:** open, see §15. Working name in docs only: "the Unweaving". It must not look like a Kigu girl in a costume.

### 7b. The Needles and the Global Tailor's Association

**The Needles lead the Global Tailor's Association** (owner, 2026-10-26), the authority over all Tailors everywhere. **They vanished
because of the entity's activity**, and the Starch Society filled the power vacuum (§6). Proposed way to keep the Grand Atelier fight
intact: three of the Four Needles (Bodkin, Darner and Tapestry) were drawn into the tapestry; Sharps was away on the road and escaped,
which is why he has been wandering Tsumugi as "Mr. Sharp" ever since, looking for them and scouting for the Guild. When the tapestry is
broken open after the climax, the three come home, and the Four Needles sit at the Grand Atelier again.

**Safe inside (owner, 2026-10-26).** The Needles have **direct contact with the Seamstresses**, and that is why they are safe inside the
tapestry: the goddesses keep them from harm the whole time. The hero's Kigu have been talking about the hero to the Seamstresses all game,
and the Seamstresses passed it on to the Needles. So when the Needles come home, they already know the hero, and they want to **formally
challenge** them so the hero can formally earn the title of **Weave Guardian** (owner's name, 2026-10-26), the champion title: the hero earns it by beating the Four
Needles and the reigning Weave Guardian, Sashiko. That is why the Four Needles battles happen at the Grand Atelier.

**The Four Needles** (Elite Four; leaders of the Global Tailor's Association, named for real needles): each is a kind of mending.

| Needle | Type | Who | Lesson | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bodkin** | Brawl | gentle giant in monk robes, eyes closed; speaks in proverbs | *Patience.* Stopped fighting after the Snarl because he couldn't untie one knot fast enough | "Breathe in. Thread through." |

*"Every tear tells a story. Mend it where everyone can see."* After the fight she hands over the **Golden Needle** and asks the hero to be the next Mender.

## 12. Naming conventions

* **Places** are sewing/textile puns: *Button, Thimble, Gingham, Hem-line, Seam-stead, Bobbin, Kite, Woolen, Loom-haven, Sel-vage, Darn-...* Parts of a garment for towns (hem, seam, cuff, yoke, gusset, selvage); fabrics for
  woods and fields (gingham, tweed, flannel, tulle); tools for landmarks (bobbin, loom, spindle, shuttle); mending words for sad or kind places (darn, patch, mend). Roads are *seam roads*; routes carry a number and a soft name ("Route 1 Meadow Lane").
* **Proposed names** (not yet on any map): Spindle Shrine (Gingham Woods), Spark Cellar (cave), Selvage (final city), the Last Seam (BIBLE: Victory Road), the Wall of Stitches (BIBLE: Hall of Fame), the Everspool, the Great Loom, the Great Press, Crisp & Co.
* **People.** Kids: short Japanese-style given names (Mimi, Noa, Ren, Yuki). The protagonists are Dahlia and Draco. Adults: a sewing word or a cosy English name (Poppy, Bryn, Zip, Gusset, Dart, Purl, Rue, Brocade, Sashiko).
  Society: pressing words (Damask, Crease, Serge, Calender, Pleat, Presser). Trainers on routes: one short first name, no surname (Lia, Bo, Suzu).
* **Kigu** names: short cute portmanteaus, a *-belle / -elle / -ette* for evolved forms; cries are sound-words.
* **Items:** food and craft words (Snack Cake, Wish Candy, Thread Ball, Wake Bell). **Trainer classes** are plain job words: Tailor, Picnicker, Hiker, Net Kid, Sailor, Scientist, Presser...

## 13. Continuity facts (don't contradict)

Tea Houses everywhere serve cocoa. The Starch Society's mantra is "Flat is fair." and its motto is "Perfection is just the beginning." (verbatim).
The Needles lead the Global Tailor's Association and vanished because of the entity; the Society filled the gap. The Society was benevolent
before its leaders found the tapestry and were misled. The legendary seamstresses (Astara and Anwela) speak through Kigu; they became Kigu by weaving Kigu with the Everspool. The tapestry is Everspool thread, and the big bad speaks to the Society through one loose thread of it. The big bad is
**not a Kigu**: it cannot be bonded with and the Everspool cannot sway it. The rival's dad stays pressed until Chapter 8. The hero's Mom knows more than she says (she has never mentioned why she stitches her hems with a double knot — the Loomhaven way).
