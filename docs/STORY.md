# Kigu Quest — Story Bible (owner: Writer)

*Companion to `docs/BIBLE.md` (world, roster, outline) and `docs/text.md` (how text is keyed and budgeted). Where this file and the BIBLE
disagree on a fact of the world, the BIBLE wins; where they disagree on a name I invented, this file wins. Proposed names for places the
level designers have not built yet are marked (proposed) — `docs/levels.md` is authoritative for anything on a map.*

## 1. Pitch and tone

> A land stitched together out of friendships is about to be ironed flat by the person who loves it most.
> A kid with a thimble of courage, a stiff-necked rival, a cheerful friend with a bottle of fabric softener, and eight very different
> Master Tailors have to show one grieving woman that a patch is not a flaw.

**Tone: warm, funny, cosy, with real stakes.**

* **Warm.** Everyone is kind first. Even the villains are polite, and have a reason. Nobody is humiliated for who they are.
* **Funny.** Wit comes from character and specifics (a named snack, an oddly precise hobby, a verbal tic), not from cruelty. At most one
  sewing pun per scene, and sometimes the speaker apologises for it ("...I'll see myself out").
* **Cosy.** Tea, cocoa, laundry on the line, lamplight, festival ribbons, the clack of a loom. Every town has a place to sit down.
* **Real stakes.** Pressed Kigu with dimmed eyes. A festival about to be flattened. A family split for thirty years. The fear is quiet, never gory.
* **All-ages.** No fanservice, no gore, no death (Kigu "fall asleep"; "lost" means "separated", and everyone is found). No swearing
  ("darn" is the strongest word in Tsumugi). Never snark at the player; teasing is for friends and only goes one way: up.
* **The player never speaks.** They nod (yes/no prompts). Other characters talk *to* them: "you", or {player} when narrating.
  Never gender the player (no he/she); Kigu are always "she", Tomo is "he", Mimi is "she".

**Craft rules for every line:** (1) vary rhythm — a short line after a long one; (2) the last line of a box carries the joke or the feeling;
(3) be specific ("a lemon macaron", not "a snack"); (4) one verbal tic per character, used sparingly; (5) people talk about what they
*do*, not about the plot; (6) don't explain the joke; (7) 2 lines per box, ~75 characters — if it doesn't fit, cut, don't cram.

## 2. Themes (and where you can see them)

1. **Individuality vs. uniformity.** The land is a *patchwork*; the Starch Society wants a *plain white weave*. Every Kigu costume is
   different; a Pressed Kigu's is bleached white with blue piping. The hero ends with eight *different* Buttons on one jacket.
2. **Bonds you choose.** Kigu *choose* their Tailors (never captured: she shakes the spool because she is deciding). Tomo and Mimi choose to stay
   friends. The twins choose. The Society's sin is taking the choosing away.
3. **Mending, not erasing.** You don't iron out a tear, you mend it — visibly, proudly (the Grand Tailor's coat is all patches). The villain is not
   destroyed; she is *invited to sew again*. Pressing is a fine tool for a seam; it is a terrible philosophy for a world.

Motif kit: buttons, threads, patches, tea, tape measures, lanterns, white vs. colour, "where everyone can see" (visible mending).

## 3. The world in one minute

**Tsumugi** ("spun thread") is a patchwork land joined by *seam roads*. **Kigu** are spirit-girls who wear *living costumes* (animal, plant,
object onesies that grow more elaborate as they evolve). A person who forms a **Stitch Bond** with a Kigu is a **Tailor**. The
**Tailors' Guild** licenses Tailors, runs the eight **Salons** (each led by a **Master Tailor**, who gives a **Button**), and crowns the
Grand Tailor at the **Grand Atelier**. The **Starch Society** believes the world should be pressed flat.

**Timeline.** *Long ago:* the twin weavers Warpa and Weftie weave the land (§7). *Thirty years ago:* **the Great Snarl** at Loomhaven's Lantern
Festival (§6). *Now:* the hero is 12 or 13, turns apprentice, and the Society is weeks from its big day: **this year's Lantern Festival.**

## 4. The cast

### The hero ({player}, boy or girl)
Lives with Mom in Button Town; the red-and-white jacket and the satchel. Wants "a Kigu of my own, someday" — and gets one on page one. A steady
needle: the hero's gift is not power but *noticing and stopping to help*, which is exactly what a Stitch Bond needs. **Arc:** apprentice → the person who
mends the land. Never speaks; the writing gives them things to be kind *to*.

### {rival} — Tomo (the rival, default name Tomo)
Serious boy, glasses, blue scarf, a notebook of everyone's battle stats. Clipped, formal, says "Hmph." (in seven moods; see §11). Tidy and disciplined because
his father taught him that discipline is love; his father is a floor manager at **Crisp & Co.** in Seamstead, and his letters home have been getting *so tidy lately*.
**Arc:** (1) Lab — takes the starter that beats yours, cocky. (2) Route 1 exit — first fight, "I'll be waiting in Thimble." (3) Thimble — lost to Poppy, is rattled that you
didn't. (4) Seamstead — defends Crisp & Co. and his dad; the raid shakes him. (5) Quarry — quieter; asks for a battle "to measure" himself; shares the letters.
(6) Kite Hill — goes looking for his father; Dart teases him until he laughs. (7) Loomhaven — finds his father pressed at the Works, frees him, holds the line
against Pressers during the climax. (8) The Last Seam — the friendly final fight: *"You were never in my way. You were the map."* He asks Prof. Bobbin to take him on.
Wins his Buttons on his own; never becomes Grand Tailor; doesn't need to.

### Mimi (the cheerful friend)
Pink cardigan, enormous bow, talks in exclamation marks, always has a spare snack. Dreams of *designing Kigu costumes*, not battling. She brings the
hero a Silk Spool in Thimble, finds the first Pressed Kigu with them in the Woods, and invents **Soft Rinse** (fabric softener, basically) — the only thing
that un-presses a Kigu. **Arc:** cheerful sidekick → the quiet hero of the Works (drives the delivery cart that opens the back gate, unpresses a hundred Kigu)
→ Loomhaven's hero the morning after: she leads the townspeople with buckets. Running gag: "Did my bow get bigger? People keep saying it got bigger."

### Professor Bobbin
Elderly, round glasses, tape-measure scarf, lab coat full of pencils. Studies Kigu ("I do not study people, which is why I am so bad at them"). Forever
searching for her tape measure (it is around her neck). Gives the starters and the Sketchbook. **Secret:** she is **Madame Damask's younger sister** (born
Bobbin; Hester took the Guild's title "Damask" when she became Guildmistress). They have not spoken since the Snarl, each believing the other blames her.
Seeds: a photo on her desk of two laughing girls, one tidy, one not; "my sister folded everything in thirds"; the Quarry is named for their family.
Reveals in Loomhaven. Her line to the hero at the start: "Go and see Tsumugi. Meet the Masters. The threads will show you the rest."

### Mom, Tomo's mom, the town
Mom bakes and heals you with cocoa. Tomo's mom worries about her husband's tidy letters. Neighbours are warm, nosy and slightly over-informed about other people's Kigu.

### "Mr. Sharp" — the recurring peddler (spoiler: Sharps, the fourth Needle)
A cheerful travelling seller in a different absurd disguise in every town (false moustache, a chef's hat, a sailor cap) who always insists he is "just a humble peddler,
nothing suspicious." Everyone sees through him; he doesn't know. He is the Guild's scout, and it is his report that makes the Grand Tailor say *yes*.
Catchphrase: "Keep your eyes sharp, friend!" (NPC id suggestion `peddler`, in Thimble, Hemline, Seamstead, the Quarry, Kite Hill, Woolen Peak, Loomhaven, the Last Seam.)

## 6. The Starch Society

**What they believe:** *Flat is fair. Crisp. Clean. Even.* A cloth that is perfectly flat cannot tangle or tear. So: no wild Kigu (they are "creases"), no tangles, no surprises. A
**Pressed** Kigu is bleached white with blue piping, calm, obedient, and says "Yes, Tailor." in a pleasant monotone. She cannot choose. (Soft Rinse undoes it.)

**How it started (their sympathetic motive).** Thirty years ago, at the **Loomhaven Lantern Festival**, a storm-wind snatched the ribbon-lanterns and knotted them through the crowd: **the Great Snarl**.
Nobody was hurt badly, but hundreds of children and Kigu were scattered for a night, and the Guildmistress — the sisters' mother — lost the strength in her hands and never sewed again. Her elder
daughter **Hester**, then a festival warden, took the Guild's title *Madame Damask* and made a vow: *never again.* Her first step was kind — "ribbon wardens" who tied every child to a parent with an
identical ribbon. The idea grew: identical ribbons, identical costumes, identical roads. Many of its members joined because they were frightened, and were *helped*. That is why it works.
Her younger sister **Bobbin** went the other way: if wild threads can tangle, study them. They have not spoken since.

**Madame Damask.** Tall, iron-grey bun, steam-iron brooch, exquisitely polite ("Do mind the creases, dear"). Never raises her voice. Pours tea. Her weakness: she has not sewn a stitch since
the Snarl (her mother's thimble is in Rue's drawer). Her plan: **re-weave Tsumugi as a plain weave** — over, under, over, under, no patterns — using the **Great Press** at the Starch Works and the
twins' threads pulled taut through the **Great Loom** in Loomhaven during the Lantern Festival: *"This year, nobody gets lost."* Her ace is the twin the hero did not save.

**The Pleats (admins)** — each with a human reason, each beaten kindly:

| Pleat | Where | Type of person | Why they joined | Tic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pleat Crease** | Gingham Woods, Seamstead | 20s, brisk, folding ruler, straightens everything she passes | Eldest of nine; the Society gave her a uniform that was *hers* and a desk that stayed tidy | "Not one crease out of place!" |
| **Pleat Serge** | Crisp & Co. (Seamstead), the Cellar | 40s, silky salesman, never stops smiling, genuinely pays fair wages | His family's tailor shop failed because every garment came out different | "Quality is consistency." |

**Pressers** (grunts, `grunt_m` / `grunt_f`): white uniforms, blue starch collars, flat caps. They say "Flat is fair!" and "Stay in line." Several are obviously homesick, one wants to bake,
one keeps folding your shirt while you battle. Class key `presser`. **Crisp & Co.:** the respectable front — linens, uniforms, "Crisp Care" cleaning. **Starch Works:** the old Woolen Peak mill.
**Public face:** "The Starch Society: A Society for Tidy Living." Its Tidy Days are folded-laundry parades.

## 7. The legend, the twins, the Needles and the Grand Tailor

**The legend of Tsumugi (as told in Loomhaven's murals and Tapestry's lullaby):** In the beginning there was only *fluff* — loose, drifting, nameless. Two sisters woke in it. **Warpa** could pull a
thread straight; **Weftie** could carry one across. "Hold this end," said Warpa. "I'll bring the other," said Weftie. Up and across they went, and where a thread crossed a thread they tied a
knot, and where they tied a knot a town grew (the first knot is Button Town — *the first button*). They dressed the wild spirits in living cloth so nobody would be lonely: the Kigu. When people
came, the sisters said: *"No thread holds by itself. Let the people and the Kigu choose one another."* That was the first Stitch Bond. When the weave was done they were tired, and lay down at the two
ends of the cloth — Warpa in a bed of embers in the north (Woolen Peak), Weftie in a bed of tides at the sea (Loomhaven harbour) — and promised to wake if the weave ever tore.

**Lullaby (Tea House, Tapestry, Loomhaven):** *"Warp goes up and weft goes by, / one holds fast and one runs wide. / Cross them once and tie them true, / and the world will hold for me and you."*

**The truth the Society missed:** a weave only holds if its threads can *give*. The Great Press works by pulling the sisters taut *apart* — the tension is what flattens the land. Reunite them, and
the tension slackens and colour returns.

**Warpa** (Dream/Ember, white-and-crimson robe, vertical threads from her sleeves): steady, patient, remembers everything. **Weftie** (Dream/Tide, white-and-blue, horizontal ribbons): curious, restless,
always wants to see what's over there. At the Works they are held in two tension frames. **The hero can free only one** (cut her thread with the Disc: Snip Poppy gave you — the first gift pays off):
the one freed takes the hero's thread in her sleeve and joins the party (suggest Lv 45ish). Damask takes the other to Loomhaven. The text supports both (`{twin}` / `{twin2}`; story keys end `.warpa` / `.weftie`).

**The Four Needles** (Elite Four; Guild seniors, named for real needles): each is a kind of mending.

| Needle | Type | Who | Lesson | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bodkin** | Brawl | gentle giant in monk robes, eyes closed; speaks in proverbs | *Patience.* Stopped fighting after the Snarl because he couldn't untie one knot fast enough | "Breathe in. Thread through." |
| **Darner** | Iron | knightly woman in thimble-plate; formal, chivalrous, cannot cook | *Mending is work, not wishing.* The Guild's auditor; she admits the Guild saw the Society rising and looked away | "No hole is too small to darn." |
| **Tapestry** | Dream | ancient, nightcap-and-robe, falls asleep mid-sentence; keeper of the lullaby | *Dreams are plans that haven't woken up yet.* The only living person who met the twins, as a child | "Mm? ...Oh, yes. Where was I?" |
| **Sharps** | Shade | masked trickster (the peddler!), grins in every disguise, turns serious on one line | *Notice things.* Being sharp is noticing, not pointing | "Keep your eyes sharp, friend!" |

**The Grand Tailor — Sashiko.** Old, small, merry; a long coat patched with hundreds of mismatched patches (each one a story; she points them out); white hair with needles stuck in the bun; calls the
hero "little stitch." Taught both sisters to sew a straight line; "I forgot to teach Hester to forgive a crooked one." Has "retired" four times. Keeps a chair for Hester at the table. Her teaching:
*"Every tear tells a story. Mend it where everyone can see."* After the fight she hands over the **Golden Needle** and asks the hero to be the next Mender.

## 9. Recurring jokes and motifs

* **Bobbin's tape measure:** she loses it while wearing it. (Payoff: Damask recognises it — *"You kept Mother's measure."*)
* **Tomo's "Hmph."** Seven moods: cocky (1), sulky (2), defensive (3), puzzled (4), proud (5), softened "Hm." (6), fond (7, final).
* **Mimi's bow** "got bigger". And her **snack pockets**: she produces the exact snack a situation needs.
* **Sign puns** (one per town, never explained): THIMBLE "Where every stitch counts." HEMLINE "We'll take you in." SEAMSTEAD "Bursting at the seams!" KITE HILL "Hold on to your hat." WOOLEN PEAK "Knit-picking welcome."
  LOOMHAVEN "A room with a loom."
* **The Tea House cocoa** is famous in every town; each town's maid has a different house special.
* **Mr. Sharp's disguises**, and the fact that everyone sees through him.
* **The apologetic pun:** a villager makes a thread pun, winces, and leaves.
* **Wrinkles are people:** a repeated lesson — someone tries to smooth something, it springs back; someone *pats* it, it settles.
* **Laundry:** Button Town's sheets dry on the sea breeze; every Society scene has somebody folding something.

## 10. Endings and credits beats

Hall of Fame (the Wall of Stitches): the Grand Tailor sews your name in. The twins appear above the Atelier's roof and weave the last seam, a thread of light from horizon to horizon; colour rolls over Tsumugi.
Credits vignettes: Poppy adds a cardigan in your size to the window; Bryn's bees make a new honey ("Everspool Blend"); Zip's marquee says "SHOWING TONIGHT: EVERYONE"; Gusset's hard hat has a bouquet;
Dart's balloon now has a patch shaped like you; Purl has finished the scarf (it is eleven metres long); Rue's Lost & Found has a new sign ("Found: Sister"); Brocade's teacup has a golden seam; Damask sews a button on a coat;
Bobbin finds her tape measure; Tomo: "Hmph. (fondly)". Mimi's costume line: **"Mimi & Co."** (no starch).

## 12. Naming conventions

* **Places** are sewing/textile puns: *Button, Thimble, Gingham, Hem-line, Seam-stead, Bobbin, Kite, Woolen, Loom-haven, Sel-vage, Darn-...* Parts of a garment for towns (hem, seam, cuff, yoke, gusset, selvage); fabrics for
  woods and fields (gingham, tweed, flannel, tulle); tools for landmarks (bobbin, loom, spindle, shuttle); mending words for sad or kind places (darn, patch, mend). Roads are *seam roads*; routes carry a number and a soft name ("Route 1 Meadow Lane").
* **Proposed names** (not yet on any map): Spindle Shrine (Gingham Woods), Spark Cellar (cave), Selvage (final city), the Last Seam (BIBLE: Victory Road), the Wall of Stitches (BIBLE: Hall of Fame), the Everspool, the Great Loom, the Great Press, Crisp & Co.
* **People.** Kids: short Japanese-style given names (Tomo, Mimi, Noa, Ren, Yuki). Adults: a sewing word or a cosy English name (Poppy, Bryn, Zip, Gusset, Dart, Purl, Rue, Brocade, Sashiko).
  Society: pressing words (Damask, Crease, Serge, Calender, Pleat, Presser). Trainers on routes: one short first name, no surname (Lia, Bo, Suzu).
* **Kigu** names: short cute portmanteaus, a *-belle / -elle / -ette* for evolved forms; cries are sound-words.
* **Items:** food and craft words (Snack Cake, Wish Candy, Thread Ball, Wake Bell). **Trainer classes** are plain job words: Tailor, Picnicker, Hiker, Net Kid, Sailor, Scientist, Presser...

## 13. Continuity facts (don't contradict)

The Snarl was 30 years ago at the Loomhaven Lantern Festival. Hester Bobbin (Madame Damask) is ~60; Prof. Bobbin ~57; Sashiko ~80. The Everspool is in the Spindle Shrine. The Society was a ribbon-warden club before it was the Society.
Nobody in the game dies. Pressed Kigu are cured by Soft Rinse (Mimi's invention) plus time. The hero's jacket ends with eight Buttons; Buttons are sewn on with the Master's thread. Kigu choose Tailors; the word "captured" is never used.
Tea Houses everywhere serve cocoa. The hero's Mom knows more than she says (she has never mentioned why she stitches her hems with a double knot — the Loomhaven way).
